Understand the scope

Shower & Bathtub Remodeling, explained clearly.

A bathing-area remodel can improve entry, comfort, water control, cleaning, and the way the room is used every day. The right scope depends on whether the existing tub or shower stays, how the drain and valves are arranged, what is found behind the surround, and whether the household values a lower entry, a deeper bath, more storage, or easier maintenance. A local professional can assess those conditions and help turn the preferred bathing experience into a complete, waterproofed installation plan.

Project paths

Start with the change that best describes your room.

Each path has its own site conditions and product decisions, but related work can be planned together under one written scope.

Path 01

Walk-In Shower Installation

A successful walk-in shower is built as a water-management system, not assembled as a collection of attractive finishes. The base, drain, waterproofing, penetrations, wall transitions, glass, and ventilation all need to work together. A local shower professional can evaluate the footprint and help compare prefabricated, custom-tile, low-threshold, and curbless approaches without losing sight of cleaning and daily comfort.

  • Footprint and drain review
  • Base and waterproofing
  • Wall and floor finish
  • Controls and spray
Path 02

Tub-to-Shower Conversion

A tub-to-shower conversion can make daily bathing feel less constrained without expanding the room. It is still a construction project: the old tub must be removed, the drain and valve assessed, walls prepared, and a complete shower and splash-control system installed. The best approach balances entry comfort, available width, drain work, storage, resale priorities, and whether another tub remains in the home.

  • Tub and surround removal
  • Alcove condition review
  • Drain and valve coordination
  • Shower base and walls
Path 03

Bathtub Replacement

Bathtub replacement can be a focused project or the centerpiece of a broader remodel. Fit is only one part of the decision: floor loading, drain and overflow alignment, faucet configuration, safe entry, wall waterproofing, access for future service, and hot-water capacity may all matter. A local provider can help narrow choices before a large or difficult-to-return fixture is ordered.

  • Tub specification review
  • Removal and access planning
  • Support and plumbing preparation
  • Tub placement and connection
Included work

Exact scope varies by room and provider. These are the workstreams commonly discussed when planning this service.

Bathing-area assessment

Review the existing footprint, entry, tub or shower condition, drain, valves, walls, subfloor, ventilation, and daily household priorities.

Removal and site preparation

Remove the agreed tub, shower, surround, glass, and fixtures while protecting adjacent finishes and checking exposed conditions.

Drain and valve coordination

Confirm drain location, supply connections, valve compatibility, service access, and any plumbing changes required by the selected fixture.

Base and waterproofing system

Prepare the substrate and install a compatible receptor, tub, tiled base, backer, membrane, transitions, and drainage details.

Walls, fixtures, and controls

Complete the wall finish, shower or tub installation, trim, spray components, niches, shelves, and accessible controls in the written scope.

Glass and splash control

Coordinate doors, panels, curtains, thresholds, slopes, and clearances so water stays within the bathing area.

Comfort and support details

Plan seating, handheld sprays, grab-bar reinforcement, ledges, anti-slip surfaces, and easier-reach storage where useful.

Testing and finish review

Test drainage, valves, seals, fixture operation, and transitions before completing a final care and maintenance walkthrough.

Choices to compare

Shape the project around your room and priorities.

  • Custom tiled base

    A site-built shower can fit unusual dimensions and finish goals but depends on exact slope, waterproofing, and tile suitability.

  • Low-threshold entry

    A reduced curb may improve access while retaining useful water containment within the available floor structure.

  • Modular shower system

    Matched base and wall panels can streamline installation and reduce grout, with style and sizing limited to available systems.

  • Custom tile conversion

    Tile offers broad design flexibility and custom niche details, with more installation steps and grout maintenance.

  • Alcove bathtub

    A wall-to-wall tub with an integrated apron is a familiar choice for tub-shower combinations and family bathrooms.

  • Freestanding tub

    An open-sided focal fixture needs enough cleaning clearance, stable flooring, coordinated filler placement, and a practical access route.

Details to confirm

Good questions prevent expensive assumptions.

Waterproofing is behind the tile

Tile and grout are finish materials; the underlying approved waterproofing system is what protects the assembly.

Glass comes late in the sequence

Custom glass is commonly measured after tile is complete, so fabrication can create a gap between surface work and final enclosure installation.

Keep resale in perspective

Some buyers value at least one bathtub, especially in family-oriented homes. The right decision depends on how long you plan to stay and what other bathrooms offer.

Windows need special treatment

A window in the tub alcove requires careful waterproofing, sill slope, privacy, material, and code review before it becomes part of a shower.

Interior dimensions vary widely

Two tubs with the same exterior length can feel different because of wall angle, ledges, overflow height, and basin width. Review specification drawings and, if possible, sit-test a display.

Refinishing is a different service

A sound tub may sometimes be refinished, but that does not correct movement, hidden leaks, poor drainage, or an unsuitable size. Compare the expected life and warranty of each approach.
