One bathing area. Every layer coordinated.

A bathing-area remodel can improve entry, comfort, water control, cleaning, and the way the room is used every day. The right scope depends on whether the existing tub or shower stays, how the drain and valves are arranged, what is found behind the surround, and whether the household values a lower entry, a deeper bath, more storage, or easier maintenance.

A finished surface is only the last layer.

The best result comes from treating the bathing area as one connected system—from the structure behind the wall to the glass at the edge.

Assess the existing area

Review the footprint, drain, valves, walls, subfloor, ventilation, and access before products are locked in.

Prepare the site

Protect adjacent finishes, remove the agreed surround, and document any exposed conditions.

Coordinate plumbing

Confirm drain position, valve compatibility, supply connections, and future service access.

Build the water system

Match the tub or base, substrate, waterproofing, transitions, penetrations, and drainage details.

Finish walls and controls

Coordinate tile or panels, trim, spray components, niches, shelves, and reachable controls.

Test the complete assembly

Check drainage, seals, glass clearances, fixture operation, and care requirements before handoff.

Good questions protect the parts you cannot see.

Confirm the hidden system, the build sequence, and the products that have to work together before removal begins.

  • Waterproofing is behind the finish

    Tile and grout are finish materials. Ask which approved system protects the substrate, penetrations, corners, and transitions.

  • Glass comes later

    Custom glass is commonly measured after surfaces are complete. Confirm fabrication time and the temporary plan before final enclosure installation.

  • Windows need their own detail

    A window in the bathing area needs deliberate waterproofing, sill slope, privacy, material, and code review.
